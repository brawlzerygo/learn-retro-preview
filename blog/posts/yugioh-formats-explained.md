In Yu-Gi-Oh!, a **format** is the game at one moment: the cards that were out, the banlist in effect and the rules of the time. A new set or banlist usually starts a new one. Players name each format after a card, a deck or the tournament that defined it, and **retro formats** bring old ones back to play today.

Format Library, a site that archives old formats and their decks, lists more than 90 for the TCG. Here are the classic ones in order, with the dates of the banlist each one uses.

## The Duel Monsters era (2002 to 2005)

| Format | Banlist | What defined it |
|---|---|---|
| Yugi-Kaiba | May 2002 | The very first: Legend of Blue Eyes White Dragon and the Yugi and Kaiba Starter Decks. A few Effect Monsters, power Spells and simple monster removal |
| Critter | July 2002 | Named after Sangan's Japanese name. The first real split between aggro and control decks |
| Treasure | October 2002 | Named after the Japanese name of the hand-disruption Spells. Magic Ruler is the newest set |
| Imperial | December 2002 | Pharaoh's Servant adds Jinzo, Nobleman of Crossout, Premature Burial, Call of the Haunted and Imperial Order |
| Android | April 2003 | Named after Jinzo's Japanese name |
| Joey-Pegasus | May 2003 | Graceful Charity, Scapegoat and Sinister Serpent arrive |
| Fiber | July 2003 | Exiled Force, Fiber Jar, Injection Fairy Lily, Yata-Garasu and Reinforcement of the Army arrive |
| Yata | August 2003 | Known for the Yata-Garasu lock that stops the opponent from drawing |
| Scientist | November 2003 | The only TCG format with three Magical Scientist |
| Vampire | February 2004 | Warrior Control takes over from Tomato Control |
| Chaos | April 2004 | Built around Black Luster Soldier - Envoy of the Beginning and Chaos Emperor Dragon - Envoy of the End |
| Warrior | October 2004 | Named after Warrior Control. The first format with a Forbidden card: Chaos Emperor Dragon |

## The GX era (2005 to 2008)

| Format | Banlist | What defined it |
|---|---|---|
| **Goat** | April 2005 | Named after Scapegoat and the Goat Control deck. One of the most popular retro formats |
| Cyber | April 2005 | Goat's banlist plus Cybernetic Revolution, which adds Cyber Dragon and Cyber End Dragon |
| Reaper | October 2005 | A banlist that hit Goat Control and combo decks. Elemental Energy and Shadow of Infinity |
| Chaos Return | April 2006 | Graceful Charity returns, and the game slows down for a while |
| Stein, Chimeratech, Demise, Trooper, Zombie, Perfect Circle, DAD Return, Gladiator | September 2006 to May 2008 | The later GX formats. Gladiator, the last, is defined by the emergency ban of Dimension Fusion |

## The ZEXAL era (2011 to 2014)

| Format | Banlist | What defined it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tengu Plant** | September 2011 | Reborn Tengu and Plant Tuners into Trishula |
| Dino Rabbit | March 2012 | |
| **Wind-Up** | September 2012 | Return of the Duelist, so some call it REDU format |
| Baby Ruler, Ravine Ruler | March and September 2013 | |

| **HAT** | April 2014 | Hands, Artifacts and Traptrix. The last format before Pendulums and before the player going first stopped drawing |

After HAT, Pendulum Monsters arrived in the TCG in July 2014, and Link Monsters in 2017.

## Which retro formats people play now

Format Library describes **Goat** and **Edison** as two of the most popular retro formats, and **Tengu Plant**, **Wind-Up** and **HAT** as some of the more popular ones. Edison draws big crowds in person: an Edison event at LDS Houston in summer 2026 drew 481 players, which TopDeck called the largest retro Yu-Gi-Oh! event ever.

Konami runs its own retro events too, called **Time Wizard**: a store picks a date, and the cards, banlist and rules match that date.
