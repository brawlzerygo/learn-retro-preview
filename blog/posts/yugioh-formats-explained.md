In Yu-Gi-Oh!, a **format** is the game at one moment: the cards that were out, the banlist in effect and the rules of the time. Every new set or banlist starts a new one. Players name each format after a card, a deck or the tournament that defined it, and **retro formats** bring old ones back to play today.

Format Library, the biggest archive of old formats, lists more than 80 for the TCG. Here are the classic ones in order, with the dates of the banlist each one uses.

## The Duel Monsters era (2002 to 2005)

| Format | Banlist | What defined it |
|---|---|---|
| Yugi-Kaiba | May 2002 | The very first: Legend of Blue Eyes and the two Starter Decks, few effect monsters, battles decide everything |
| Critter | July 2002 | Named after Sangan's Japanese name. The first real split between aggro and control decks |
| Treasure | October 2002 | Magic Ruler arrives, and with it Ritual monsters |
| Imperial | December 2002 | Pharaoh's Servant adds Jinzo, Imperial Order, Call of the Haunted and Nobleman of Crossout |
| Android | April 2003 | Named after Jinzo's Japanese name |
| Joey-Pegasus | May 2003 | Graceful Charity, Scapegoat and Sinister Serpent arrive |
| Fiber | July 2003 | Fiber Jar, Yata-Garasu and Exiled Force arrive |
| Yata | August 2003 | Known for the Yata-Garasu lock that stops the opponent from drawing |
| Scientist | November 2003 | The only TCG format with three Magical Scientist |
| Vampire | February 2004 | Warrior Control takes over from Tomato Control |
| Chaos | April 2004 | Black Luster Soldier - Envoy of the Beginning and Chaos Emperor Dragon rule the game |
| Warrior | October 2004 | Chaos Emperor Dragon is Forbidden, and Warrior Control is the deck to beat |

## The GX era (2005 to 2008)

| Format | Banlist | What defined it |
|---|---|---|
| **Goat** | April 2005 | Scapegoat and Metamorphosis into Thousand-Eyes Restrict. The most played retro format today |
| Cyber | April 2005 | Cybernetic Revolution adds Cyber Dragon |
| Reaper | October 2005 | Elemental Energy and Shadow of Infinity |
| Chaos Return | April 2006 | Enemy of Justice |
| Stein, Chimeratech, Demise, Trooper, Zombie, Perfect Circle, DAD Return, Gladiator | September 2006 to May 2008 | The later GX formats, ending with Gladiator Beasts |

## The ZEXAL era (2011 to 2014)

| Format | Banlist | What defined it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tengu Plant** | September 2011 | Reborn Tengu and Plant Tuners into Trishula |
| Dino Rabbit | March 2012 | |
| **Wind-Up** | September 2012 | Return of the Duelist, so some call it REDU format |
| Baby Ruler, Ravine Ruler | March and September 2013 | The Dragon Ruler formats |
| Fire-Water, Vegas | January and April 2014 | |
| **HAT** | April 2014 | Hands, Artifacts and Traptrix. The last format before Pendulums and before the player going first stopped drawing |

After HAT, Pendulum Summoning arrived in July 2014, and later Link Monsters, and the game started to look like it does today.

## Which retro formats people play now

Format Library marks five formats as popular: **Goat, Edison, Tengu Plant, Wind-Up and HAT**. Goat has the most logged decks and events by far, and Edison is close behind. Edison is also growing fast in person: an Edison event at LDS Houston in August 2026 drew 481 players, the biggest retro Yu-Gi-Oh! event so far.

Konami runs its own retro events too, called **Time Wizard**: a store picks a date, and the cards, banlist and rules match that date.
