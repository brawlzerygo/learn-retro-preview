Yu-Gi-Oh! never stands still. New sets come out every few months, the banlist changes with them, and the game you learn this year plays differently next year. A **retro format** picks one moment and freezes it: a fixed card pool, a fixed banlist and the rules from that time. **Edison format** is one of the most popular of them, according to Format Library, a site that archives old formats. It is Yu-Gi-Oh! as it was in April 2010.

## Where Edison comes from

Edison comes from the 5D's era of Yu-Gi-Oh!, the era of the **Synchro Monster**. Instead of drawing a big monster and paying Tributes for it, you send a Tuner and other monsters whose Levels add up to the Graveyard, and the Synchro comes straight out of your Extra Deck.

Edison sits right at the start of that era. In March 2010 a new banlist came out, and the newest set in stores was Absolute Powerforce. That moment lasted only about two and a half months, until The Shining Darkness was released.

In late April 2010, Konami held its 75th Shonen Jump Championship in Edison, New Jersey. Konami called it the biggest Shonen Jump Championship ever, and it was also the last one before the series became the Yu-Gi-Oh! Championship Series. Jeff Jones won it with a deck called Quickdraw Dandywarrior.

## Why players love it

That tournament's Top 16 was one of the most varied in Shonen Jump Championship history. Lightsworn, Gravekeepers and Cat decks all made it, right next to the Synchro decks. Years later, players chose that moment to freeze, and named the format after the town where the tournament was played.

Guides describe Edison as a middle ground between older formats and modern Yu-Gi-Oh!: powerful Synchro Monsters decide a lot of games, but decks have few set opening combos and card advantage is hard to earn.
