What Is Goat Format? Yu-Gi-Oh! as It Was in Summer 2005

November 23, 2026

Modern Yu-Gi-Oh! changes all the time. Every few months there is a new set, a new banlist and a new best deck. A retro format stops the clock at one moment and keeps it there. Goat format stops it in the summer of 2005: the cards, the banlist and the rules of that time, and nothing newer.

Where the name comes from

The name comes from Scapegoat, the Quick-Play Spell that summons four Sheep Tokens. It was so popular in 2005 that players named the format after it. The signature deck, Goat Control, paired it with Metamorphosis, which Tributes a token to summon Thousand-Eyes Restrict from the Fusion Deck.

Konami doesn't run Goat format. Players keep it going themselves, with their own card pool, rulings and events, including a yearly Goat Format World Championship.

What is in the card pool

The community standard uses every card that was legal at premier events on August 17, 2005. That takes in sets up to The Lost Millennium, plus the promos of that time. Exarion Universe and Cybernetic Revolution are left out.

The banlist is the one from April 2005, and it never changes. Cards like Monster Reborn, Dark Hole, Raigeki and Change of Heart are Forbidden. Cards like Pot of Greed, Graceful Charity and Black Luster Soldier - Envoy of the Beginning are Limited to one copy.

How it plays

  • No Synchros, Xyz, Pendulums or Links. The only monsters that come from outside your Main Deck are Fusions, kept in what was then called the Fusion Deck.
  • Slow and careful. Games are control-heavy. Getting more than one card ahead in a single turn is rare, and when it happens it can decide the game.
  • Reading your opponent matters. A lot of the skill is working out what your opponent is holding and playing around it.
  • The card pool never moves. A deck you build today stays legal forever, and there is no new set to keep up with.

A few rules work the 2005 way

Most of the game works the way it does today. A handful of things don't. Goat rulings follow how a judge would have ruled in 2005, the player going first still draws a card on their first turn, and a monster flipped face-down by a card effect can be flipped up again that same turn. Our course covers each of these where it comes up, so you don't need to memorize them up front.

How to start playing

  • Learn the game step by step: the free Goat course starts from the very basics, for players who never played or haven't played in years.
  • Look up any card: every Goat-legal card has a page with its text and banlist status, in the Goat card search.
  • Pick a deck: start with the Goat deck lists. YGOPRODeck's guide recommends Goat Control for new players.
  • Play online: DuelingBook has a Goat Format room, and EDOPro, a free simulator, has the Goat banlist built in.
