Yu-Gi-Oh! Formats Explained, in Order (2002 to 2014)

November 23, 2026

In Yu-Gi-Oh!, a format is the game at one moment: the cards that were out, the banlist in effect and the rules of the time. A new set or banlist usually starts a new one. Players name each format after a card, a deck or the tournament that defined it, and retro formats bring old ones back to play today.

Format Library, a site that archives old formats and their decks, lists more than 90 for the TCG. Here are the classic ones in order, with the dates of the banlist each one uses.

The Duel Monsters era (2002 to 2005)

FormatBanlistWhat defined it
Yugi-KaibaMay 2002The very first: Legend of Blue Eyes White Dragon and the Yugi and Kaiba Starter Decks. A few Effect Monsters, power Spells and simple monster removal
CritterJuly 2002Named after Sangan's Japanese name. The first real split between aggro and control decks
TreasureOctober 2002Named after the Japanese name of the hand-disruption Spells. Magic Ruler is the newest set
ImperialDecember 2002Pharaoh's Servant adds Jinzo, Nobleman of Crossout, Premature Burial, Call of the Haunted and Imperial Order
AndroidApril 2003Named after Jinzo's Japanese name
Joey-PegasusMay 2003Graceful Charity, Scapegoat and Sinister Serpent arrive
FiberJuly 2003Exiled Force, Fiber Jar, Injection Fairy Lily, Yata-Garasu and Reinforcement of the Army arrive
YataAugust 2003Known for the Yata-Garasu lock that stops the opponent from drawing
ScientistNovember 2003The only TCG format with three Magical Scientist
VampireFebruary 2004Warrior Control takes over from Tomato Control
ChaosApril 2004Built around Black Luster Soldier - Envoy of the Beginning and Chaos Emperor Dragon - Envoy of the End
WarriorOctober 2004Named after Warrior Control. The first format with a Forbidden card: Chaos Emperor Dragon

The GX era (2005 to 2008)

FormatBanlistWhat defined it
GoatApril 2005Named after Scapegoat and the Goat Control deck. One of the most popular retro formats
CyberApril 2005Goat's banlist plus Cybernetic Revolution, which adds Cyber Dragon and Cyber End Dragon
ReaperOctober 2005A banlist that hit Goat Control and combo decks. Elemental Energy and Shadow of Infinity
Chaos ReturnApril 2006Graceful Charity returns, and the game slows down for a while
Stein, Chimeratech, Demise, Trooper, Zombie, Perfect Circle, DAD Return, GladiatorSeptember 2006 to May 2008The later GX formats. Gladiator, the last, is defined by the emergency ban of Dimension Fusion

After HAT, Pendulum Monsters arrived in the TCG in July 2014, and Link Monsters in 2017.

Which retro formats people play now

Format Library describes Goat and Edison as two of the most popular retro formats, and Tengu Plant, Wind-Up and HAT as some of the more popular ones. Edison draws big crowds in person: an Edison event at LDS Houston in summer 2026 drew 481 players, which TopDeck called the largest retro Yu-Gi-Oh! event ever.

Konami runs its own retro events too, called Time Wizard: a store picks a date, and the cards, banlist and rules match that date.
