What Is Edison Format? Yu-Gi-Oh! as It Was in April 2010

November 22, 2026

Yu-Gi-Oh! never stands still. New sets come out every few months, the banlist changes with them, and the game you learn this year plays differently next year. A retro format picks one moment and freezes it: a fixed card pool, a fixed banlist and the rules from that time. Edison format is one of the most popular of them. It is Yu-Gi-Oh! as it was in April 2010.

Where Edison comes from

In 2008, Konami launched Yu-Gi-Oh! 5D's, and with it a new kind of monster: the Synchro Monster. Instead of drawing a big monster and paying Tributes for it, you combine a Tuner with other monsters whose Levels add up, and the Synchro comes straight out of your Extra Deck.

Edison sits right at the start of that era. In March 2010 a new banlist came out, and the newest set in stores was Absolute Powerforce. That moment lasted only about two and a half months, until The Shining Darkness was released.

In late April 2010, Konami held its 75th Shonen Jump Championship in Edison, New Jersey. Konami called it the biggest Shonen Jump Championship ever, and it was also the last one before the series became the Yu-Gi-Oh! Championship Series. Jeff Jones won it with a deck called Quickdraw Dandywarrior.

Why players love it

That tournament's Top 16 was one of the most varied in Shonen Jump Championship history. Lightsworn, Gravekeepers and Cat decks all made it, right next to the Synchro decks. Years later, players chose that moment to freeze, and named the format after the town where the tournament was played.

Many players see Edison as a sweet spot between two eras. It is faster and more explosive than older formats thanks to Synchro Monsters, but still slow enough that every card and every choice matters.
