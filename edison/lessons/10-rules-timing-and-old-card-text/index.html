Rules, Timing and Old Card Text

Priority, the Chain order, the Damage Step, 2010 card text, and the cards that answer a Synchro. 12 min · 12 flashcards

Why this lesson

Edison 101 taught you enough to play a full game. This lesson adds the finer rules. Edison uses the rules from 2010, and a few of them are different from today's game. Online card text often shows the modern rules, which can confuse you. Take them one at a time.

1. Priority

When you summon a monster, you get the first chance to act. If the summon did not start a Chain, you can activate an Ignition Effect before your opponent responds.

YouUse priority. Activate Chaos Sorcerer's effect and banish their monster.
OpponentNow they can respond, for example with . Your effect is already on the Chain.

This also works with Synchros. Synchro Summon , then use its effect right away with priority, before your opponent can Bottomless it.

When you lose priority

If the summon starts a Chain, you lose priority. Example: you use as material for Brionac. Sangan's effect triggers and starts a Chain. Now you cannot use Brionac's Ignition Effect first.

2. Two things trigger at once

Sometimes two effects trigger at the same time. They go on the Chain in this order:

  1. Turn player's mandatory effects
  2. Opponent's mandatory effects
  3. Turn player's optional effects
  4. Opponent's optional effects

Example: you Tribute to summon . Sangan's effect is mandatory, so it is Chain Link 1. Caius is Chain Link 2.

3. Missing the timing

An optional effect that says "When ... you can" misses its timing if something else happened last. Effects that say "If" never miss the timing. Read the wording on each card.

4. The Damage Step is a narrow window

The Damage Step starts when an attack is about to hit. Very few cards can be used here.

  • Cards that change ATK or DEF, before damage is calculated
  • Counter Traps
  • Some monster effects made for this moment, like and

Most Spells and Traps cannot be used. Spell Speed 2 cards that negate cannot be used here at all.

Think of it like this

The Damage Step is the moment two boxers swing. You can still change how hard they hit. You cannot walk into the ring with a new plan.

5. Two smaller rules

  • One Field Spell for the whole field. Only one Field Spell is in play at a time, shared by both players. When a new one resolves, the old one is destroyed. Today, each player has their own Field Zone.
  • Life Points. You cannot pay a cost that takes your LP to 0. If you have 800 LP, you cannot pay 800 LP for a cost.

The first-turn draw, the other rule people mix up, is in Lesson 2. Every rule is on the Rules page.

6. Old card text

Over the years, Konami rewrote some cards to make them weaker or clearer. This is called errata. When the rewrite changes how the card works, it is functional errata. Edison uses the text from 2010, but most websites and online tools show today's text. So a card you look up can say something different from how it works in Edison.

Think of it like this

It is like playing a classic board game with the original rulebook. A newer edition changed some rules. Your table still uses the old book.

7. Answering a Synchro: the two moments

In Lesson 7 you learned that a failed Synchro costs the monster and its materials. Here is how the answers work on a Chain. Your opponent can stop a Synchro at two different moments.

Moment 1: while it is happening

Some cards negate the summon itself. The monster never really lands.

Half your LPLimited (1)
Tribute 1 monsterUnlimited
800 LP each timeSemi-Limited (2)

When a Synchro Summon is negated:

  • The materials are already in your Graveyard. They do not come back.
  • The Synchro is destroyed and goes to the Graveyard.
  • It was not properly summoned, so it cannot be revived later.
  • Effects like Black Rose Dragon's board wipe never happen.
Royal Oppression is special

Royal Oppression stays on the field. As long as it is there, either player can pay 800 LP to stop any Special Summon. One copy can shut down a whole Synchro deck for many turns.

Moment 2: right after it lands

Other cards let the summon happen, then destroy or banish the monster: , , and later, when it attacks, or . The monster did land, so any effect it started when it was summoned can still resolve.

An example: Black Rose meets Bottomless

The dragon is gone, but its blast still happens. The effect was already activated, so removing the monster does not stop it. That is why Bottomless is a weak answer to Black Rose.

Common mistakes
  • Letting your opponent respond before you use priority.
  • Trying to activate Mirror Force in the Damage Step.
  • Using Brionac only once per turn, or thinking Goyo Guardian needs an EARTH Tuner, because the modern text says so.
  • Thinking Bottomless stops Black Rose's effect. It does not.
  • Forgetting that you can also pay for Royal Oppression. It works for both players.

Recap

  • Priority: after a summon that starts no Chain, you can use an Ignition Effect first.
  • Triggers at the same time go on the Chain in a set order: mandatory before optional, turn player first.
  • "When ... you can" can miss the timing. "If" cannot.
  • The Damage Step allows only ATK/DEF changes, Counter Traps, and a few monster effects.
  • One Field Spell for the whole field. No cost can take you to 0 LP.
  • Edison uses 2010 card text: Brionac, Goyo and Treeborn Frog are stronger than today.
  • Negation (Solemn Judgment, Horn of Heaven, Royal Oppression) stops the summon itself. Bottomless and Torrential hit it after it lands, so its summon effect can still resolve.
