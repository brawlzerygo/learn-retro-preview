Playing Around Traps

What each trap needs, and how to read and bluff with Set cards. 5 min · 6 flashcards

Every trap has a trigger

A trap can't be used whenever your opponent wants. Each one needs a certain event to happen first. If you know the event, you can choose not to give it to them.

Two rules from earlier lessons
  • A Trap can't be activated the turn it is Set. lets your opponent Set a card during your turn, but that card can't be used until a later turn.
  • Some threats sit in the hand, not on the field. (26 of 55 lists) comes out when its owner takes damage while controlling no cards.

Making plays a trap can't punish

You can't play around every trap at once. Pick the one that would hurt most, and give your opponent the worst possible use of it.

  • Attack with your least important monster first. Mirror Force and Dimensional Prison both trigger on an attack. Let them hit the monster you care about least.
  • Keep monsters in Defense Position when you won't attack. Mirror Force only destroys Attack Position monsters.
  • Summon a small monster first. A monster under 1500 ATK can't be hit by Bottomless Trap Hole. If they use Torrential Tribute on it, you lose less.
  • Don't overextend. Torrential Tribute destroys every monster. Two strong monsters are often enough. A third one is only more to lose.
  • Remove Set cards one at a time. If your opponent has 2 or more Set cards, gives Starlight Road a target. only destroys one card, so Road can't respond.
  • Think before an empty-field attack. If your opponent controls no cards, your damage can bring out Gorz. Make sure you can handle a 2700 ATK monster.
Remove first, then play

Spell and Trap removal is everywhere: is in 54 of 55 lists, Heavy Storm in 47, Dust Tornado in 40. Clear a Set card before your big play when you can.

Reading Set cards

A Set card isn't a mystery. Your opponent's choices give you clues. These are ways to think, not sure answers.

  1. Count them. One Set card limits what can go wrong. Three Set cards means several threats, or some bluffs.
  2. Watch what they let through. If a big Summon resolved with no response, Solemn Judgment, Bottomless and Torrential are less likely. They may also be saving one.
  3. Watch what they protected. If they used MST on your card instead of holding it, their own Set card may not be a Quick-Play.
  4. Check the GY and the banlist. Mirror Force, Solemn Judgment and Torrential Tribute are Limited. If one is already in the GY, there are no more.
  5. Test with a cheap play. Attack with a weak monster or Summon a small one. Their answer tells you what they hold.

Bluffing

Your opponent reads your Set cards the same way. You can use that.

  • Set cards you can't use yet. A Set card that does nothing can still make your opponent slow down.
  • Set Quick-Plays. and MST can be Set like Traps. Your opponent can't tell which is which.
  • Act the same every time. If you pause only when you hold a trap, your opponent learns to read the pause.
Common mistake

Being too scared of Set cards. If you never attack into one, your opponent's bluff wins the game for free. Play around the one trap that would hurt most, then make your play.

Recap

  • Each trap needs a trigger: an attack, a Summon, a Special Summon, or 2 or more cards being destroyed.
  • Attack with your least important monster first, and summon small before big.
  • Use MST, not Heavy Storm, when Starlight Road might be Set.
  • Read Set cards by what your opponent did, and Set your own Quick-Plays to bluff.
