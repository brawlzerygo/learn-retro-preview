The Staples

The cards you will see in almost every game, and the mistake they punish. 10 min · 8 flashcards

What a staple is

A staple is a card that shows up in deck after deck, whatever the deck's plan. Staples are strong on their own, so almost everyone plays them.

The numbers below come from the 55 deck lists on this site. A card counts if a deck runs it anywhere: Main, Extra or Side Deck. You don't need to learn every card in the format to start. Learn these, and you will know most of what your opponent can do.

Think of it like this

Staples are the salt and pepper of Edison. Every cook uses them, whatever the dish.

1. Spell and Trap removal

These destroy your opponent's Spells and Traps, often before they can be used.

2. Traps that stop attacks

These wait face-down until your opponent attacks.

CardIn decksBanlistDescription
78%1When an opponent's monster declares an attack, destroy all their Attack Position monsters.
56%3When an opponent's monster declares an attack, banish that monster.

3. Traps that stop summons

These hit a monster as it arrives. They hurt most against a Synchro Summon. If one of them stops your Synchro, you lose the Synchro and the monsters you used to make it. That is two or more cards gone for one Trap.

CardIn decksBanlistDescription
73%1Pay half your LP to stop a summon, or a Spell or Trap, and destroy it.
65%2When your opponent summons a monster with 1500 or more ATK, destroy it and banish it.
65%1When a monster is summoned, destroy every monster on the field.

4. Answers to a big monster

When your opponent has a monster you can't beat in battle, these deal with it.

The most common mistake

New players make the same mistake again and again: they Synchro Summon into a wall of Set cards. Your opponent has three cards face-down. You make your best Synchro anyway. A Bottomless Trap Hole or Solemn Judgment stops it, and you lose three cards for their one.

Set cards are a warning

Every face-down Spell or Trap could be one of the staples above. Before you commit your best monsters, deal with them or test them.

How to play around it

  1. Count their Set cards. More Set cards means more risk.
  2. Clear first. Use , or before you summon your best monster.
  3. Bait with a smaller summon. Summon something you can afford to lose first. If they use a Trap on it, your next summon is safer.
  4. Know the limits. A deck can run only 1 Solemn Judgment, 1 Torrential Tribute and 2 Bottomless Trap Hole. If one has already been used, the risk goes down.
Common beginner mistakes
  • Making your best Synchro into 3 Set cards when you have Heavy Storm in hand.
  • Attacking with every monster into a Set card when one of them could run into Mirror Force.
  • Forgetting that Heavy Storm destroys your own Set cards too.

Recap

  • A staple is a card in deck after deck.
  • Spell and Trap removal: Mystical Space Typhoon, Heavy Storm, Dust Tornado.
  • Traps that stop attacks: Mirror Force, Dimensional Prison.
  • Traps that stop summons: Solemn Judgment, Bottomless Trap Hole, Torrential Tribute.
  • Answers to a big monster: Brain Control, Book of Moon.
  • Staple monsters: Cyber Dragon, D.D. Crow, Gorz, Sangan.
  • Before a big Synchro: count Set cards, clear them, bait them, and know the limits.
