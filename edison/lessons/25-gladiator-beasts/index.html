Gladiator Beasts

Attack, then swap your monster for a fresh one from the Deck. 5 min · 6 flashcards

At a glance: Gladiator Beasts

Control · Lists: Gladiator Beast · Lesson 25

How to spot it

A Gladiator Beast attacks, then shuffles itself into the Deck at the end of the Battle Phase and a different one comes out with a bonus effect.

What it wants

Win a battle, tag out for a fresh Gladiator Beast with a bonus, and build to or .

Strengths

  • Each tag out is a free monster with a free effect.
  • negates a monster effect while they control a Gladiator Beast.
  • Shrink, Book of Moon and Dimensional Prison protect its battles.

Weaknesses

  • It needs to battle: banishes the attacker before damage, so it never tags out.
  • The whole engine is Special Summons: and stop it.
  • makes a monster forget it was summoned by a Gladiator Beast.

What to expect

  • swapping a Gladiator Beast without a battle.
  • destroying a Spell or Trap, a monster.
  • Heraklinos negating any Spell or Trap for a discard.

How to beat it

  • Make it attack into your Traps instead of attacking into it.
  • Hit a Normal Summoned Gladiator Beast before it battles: it has no bonus.
  • Use Spells and Traps against War Chariot, since it only negates monster effects.

Don't

  • Attacking into a Gladiator Beast with a monster it can beat: every win is a tag out.
  • Using a monster effect while War Chariot is Set.

The idea: tag out after battle

Almost every Gladiator Beast has the same second effect. At the end of the Battle Phase, if it battled, you can shuffle it into your Deck. Then you Special Summon a different Gladiator Beast from your Deck.

That new monster is "Special Summoned by the effect of a Gladiator Beast monster." This matters. Each Gladiator Beast has a first effect that only works when it arrives this way.

Think of it like this

A tag team in wrestling. One fighter steps in, takes the hit, then tags a partner who jumps in fresh. The partner gets a bonus move on the way in.

The Gladiator Beast list is a Rogue deck in our data: playable and dangerous, but the odds are against it. Open the Gladiator Beast list to follow along.

Edison rulings for the tag out
  • "Battled" means the monster was in damage calculation. If its attack is negated, it can't tag out.
  • You need a Gladiator Beast left in your Deck to use it.
  • can negate it. The monster has already been shuffled into the Deck, so nothing comes out.
  • does not stop it.

Swapping without battle, and Contact Fusion

is the deck's shortcut. If you control a face-up Gladiator Beast, you can Special Summon Tiger from your hand. Then Tribute Tiger to shuffle a Gladiator Beast back into the Deck and summon another. The new one counts as summoned by a Gladiator Beast effect, so its bonus works. You can even do this in your Main Phase, before you attack.

How to play against it

  • Stop the battle. banishes the attacker before damage calculation, so it never battles and never tags out.
  • Stop the summon. and stop Special Summons. Against Gladiator Beasts, that stops the whole engine.
  • Set it face-down. makes a monster "forget" it was summoned by a Gladiator Beast. Darius also stops targeting its revived monster.
  • Mind War Chariot. While they control a Gladiator Beast, your monster effects can be negated. Spells and Traps get around it, but Heraklinos can negate those.
  • A Normal Summon gets no bonus. A Gladiator Beast that was Normal Summoned has no first effect. Hit it before it battles.
Common mistake

Attacking into a Gladiator Beast with a monster it can beat. Every battle it wins also gives it a tag out. Make it attack into your traps instead.

Recap

  • A Gladiator Beast that battled can shuffle itself back and summon another at the end of the Battle Phase.
  • The new one gets a bonus effect because a Gladiator Beast summoned it.
  • Test Tiger swaps without battle. Prisma helps make Gyzarus.
  • Stop it by stopping the battle or the Special Summon.
