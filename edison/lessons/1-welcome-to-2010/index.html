What a retro format is

Yu-Gi-Oh! never stands still. New sets come out every few months, and the banlist changes with them. The game you learn this year won't quite be the game you play next year.

A retro format picks one moment and freezes it: a fixed card pool, a fixed banlist, and the rules from that time. The game stays the same long enough to really learn, and it is full of decks many players remember. Goat format, from 2005, is one you may have heard of. Edison is another.

The story

Every few months a new set brought new cards, and over the years the game kept changing.

In 2008, Konami launched a new show, Yu-Gi-Oh! 5D's. With it came a brand-new kind of monster: the Synchro Monster. It changed how people played.

Edison sits right at the start of that Synchro era. In March 2010 a new banlist came out, and the newest set in stores was Absolute Powerforce. That moment only lasted about two and a half months, until the next set, The Shining Darkness, came out.

In late April 2010, Konami held its 75th Shonen Jump Championship in Edison, New Jersey. Konami called it the biggest Shonen Jump Championship of all time. It was also the last one: the series was relaunched as the Yu-Gi-Oh! Championship Series (YCS). Jeff Jones won it with a deck called Quickdraw Dandywarrior, which you'll meet in Lesson 20.

What made the tournament special was its Top 16, one of the most varied in Shonen Jump Championship history. Lightsworn, Gravekeepers and Cat decks all made it, next to the Synchro decks. You didn't need the single best deck to win.

Years later, players decided to freeze that moment. They said: "Let's play with only the cards and rules from April 2010. Nothing newer." That frozen moment is Edison format, named after the town where the tournament was played.

Many players see Edison as a sweet spot. Synchro Monsters make it fast and explosive, but it is still slow enough that every card and every choice matters. That makes it a nice break from how fast the modern game plays.

Think of it like this

Edison is a time capsule from 2010. You open it and play the game exactly as it was that spring.

Sources: Konami's SHONEN JUMP Championship blog (SJC History: The Future) and YGOPRODeck's introduction to SJC Edison format.

The big idea: Synchro Monsters

Before Synchros, a strong monster, Level 5 or higher, had to be in your hand first. You had to draw it, then pay one or two Tributes to Tribute Summon it. You could wait several turns for the right card.

Synchro Monsters don't live in your Deck at all. They wait in a separate pile called the Extra Deck, where you can always reach them. Once you have the right monsters on the field, you can bring one out. So big monsters show up far more often.

Think of baking. Flour alone is not a cake. Eggs alone are not a cake. Mix them together, and you get something much better than either one.

Edison works the same way. You take two monsters on your field and combine them into one big, powerful monster from your Extra Deck.

The big idea

In Edison, almost every monster can become part of a bigger monster.

Why this matters to you

A small monster is worth its effect, like , which adds a small monster from your Deck to your hand when it goes from the field to the Graveyard. In Edison it is also worth its Level, because its Level lets it become part of a bigger monster.

So you don't only ask: "Can my monster beat their monster?"

You also ask: "What can my opponent turn their monsters into?"

Two weak monsters on the field can be a real threat. That is the main way of thinking in Edison. Everything else in this course builds on it.

What Edison 101 covers

Edison 101 treats Edison as your first format. You learn how a match works and how to read card text, then Synchro Summoning, the Extra Deck and tokens, the staple cards you will meet in every game, the banlist, and finally a full first game, turn by turn. If you have never played at all, Step 0 teaches the basic rules first.

Recap

  • A retro format freezes one moment of the game. Edison freezes April 2010.
  • It is named after the 75th Shonen Jump Championship, in Edison, New Jersey, the last one before the YCS.
  • Synchro Monsters are the big idea. They wait in the Extra Deck.
  • A small monster is worth its effect and its Level.
