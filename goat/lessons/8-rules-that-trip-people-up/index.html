Rules That Trip People Up

Priority, triggers on a Chain, the Damage Step, replays and the June 2005 rule. 9 min · 6 flashcards

Goat is played the way a head judge ruled it in 2005. Most rules match the modern game, but a handful don't, and they decide games. These are the ones to know.

Priority: the turn player goes first

When a summon or a Chain finishes, the turn player gets the first chance to do something next. They can use a fast card, or a monster's Ignition effect, before the opponent gets a turn to respond.

Why it matters

You Normal Summon . Thanks to priority, you can remove its counter and aim at a Set card right away. Your opponent never gets a window to turn Breaker face-down with Book of Moon before its effect starts.

Effects that trigger at the same time

Sometimes two or more effects trigger from one event, for example when Mirror Force destroys two monsters that each have a "when destroyed" effect. In Goat they go on the Chain in the order their events happened, earliest first. The last link resolves first, as always.

The Damage Step

The Damage Step is the stretch of an attack where the damage is worked out. In 2005 it is split into six parts, and each part allows only one Chain.

The big difference: during the Damage Step, the only cards you can use are monster effects that negate an activation. No Quick-Play Spells, no Normal Traps, no Continuous Trap effects. If you want to stop an attack with or a Trap, do it when the attack is declared, not after.

A common slip

Waiting to see whether the attacker flips your Set monster before you respond. By then you are in the Damage Step, and your Traps are locked out.

Replays

If the number of monsters your opponent controls changes after you declare an attack, for example because brings one back, you get a replay: choose a new target or stop the attack. In Goat, a replay counts as a brand-new attack declaration, so cards that respond to an attack being declared can be used again.

The June 2005 position rule

Before June 2005, a monster turned face-down by a card effect couldn't be flipped back up that turn, because the move counted as its one position change. After the change, it no longer counts. Standard Goat uses the newer rule.

That is what lets a deck turn its own face-down with or and use its Flip effect again. YGOPRODeck's introduction points to this rule as the reason Goat Control works at all.

Smaller rules

  • Two 0 ATK monsters that battle in Attack Position are both destroyed.
  • A Life Point cost bigger than what you have left can't be paid.
  • Only one face-up Field Spell exists for the whole table.

Recap

  • After a summon or a Chain, the turn player acts first.
  • Effects that trigger together go on the Chain in the order they happened.
  • In the Damage Step only monster effects that negate an activation can be used.
  • A replay is a new attack declaration.
  • Being turned face-down by a card doesn't use up a monster's position change.
