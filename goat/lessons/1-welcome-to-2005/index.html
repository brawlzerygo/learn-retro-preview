Welcome to 2005

What Goat format is, where the name comes from, and why people still play it. 6 min · 5 flashcards

What is a format?

A format is a set of rules for which cards you may use. Today's official game adds new cards every few months and changes its banlist often. A retro format does the opposite: it freezes the game at one moment in the past, with the cards and rules of that time, and keeps it there.

Goat: the game as it stood in 2005

Goat format is Yu-Gi-Oh! as it was played at big tournaments in the summer of 2005. The cards are the ones released up to that point, and the banlist is the official one from April 2005. Konami no longer supports it. Players run it themselves, with their own events and rules site.

Where the name comes from

The format is named after , a Quick-Play Spell that puts four Sheep Tokens on your field at once. It was everywhere in 2005. The most popular deck of the time, Goat Control, paired it with : send one Sheep Token away and bring out a Level 1 Fusion, , which can take your opponent's monster for itself.

Think of it like this

Four sheep that can't attack sound useless. In Goat they are shields, Tributes and Fusion fuel all at once. That is the kind of puzzle this format is full of.

Why people still play it

  • Every card counts. Games are slower and more careful than modern Yu-Gi-Oh!. Winning usually comes from making good trades and ending up with more cards than your opponent.
  • It never changes. No new sets, no new banlists. Once you learn it, your knowledge keeps its value.
  • Many decks can play. Control decks, aggressive Warrior decks, Burn and more all have a place.
  • It is cheap to build. Most decks cost little next to a modern one, with a few pricey exceptions like .
  • It is alive. The community runs regular tournaments, including a yearly World Championship, and plays online on DuelingBook.

Recap

  • A retro format freezes the card pool and rules at one point in time.
  • Goat is the summer of 2005, with the April 2005 banlist.
  • It is named after Scapegoat and the Goat Control deck built around it.
  • People play it for its careful, card-by-card games, its fixed pool and its active community.
