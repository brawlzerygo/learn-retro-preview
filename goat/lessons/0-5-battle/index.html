Battle

Positions, attacking, the damage math and direct attacks. 8 min · 6 flashcards

Played Yu-Gi-Oh! before? You can skip the basics.Go to Goat 101 →

Two positions

A monster sits upright in Attack Position or sideways in Defense Position. Upright, its ATK counts. Sideways, its DEF counts when it is attacked.

In a Main Phase, you may switch a face-up monster between the two, once per turn. Not on the turn it arrived, though.

Declaring an attack

In your Battle Phase, each of your Attack Position monsters can attack once. You pick one of your monsters and one of the opponent's monsters to hit. Defense Position monsters can't attack.

The math

Only the attacker's ATK matters on your side. On the other side, it depends on the defender's position.

DefenderResultDescription
Attack PositionHigher ATK winsThe weaker one is destroyed, and its owner loses LP equal to the gap. A tie destroys both, and no one loses LP.
Defense PositionATK against DEFIf ATK is higher, the defender is destroyed but its owner loses no LP. If DEF is higher, the attacker's owner loses the gap. A tie changes nothing.
Worked example

, 2500 ATK, attacks in Attack Position, 1000 ATK. Sangan is destroyed and its owner loses 1500 LP.

Now Summoned Skull attacks in Defense Position, 2600 DEF. Nothing is destroyed, and the attacker's owner loses 100 LP.

Attacking a face-down monster

If you attack a Set monster, it is turned face-up first and then the battle is worked out with its DEF. Any Flip effect on it triggers, which is the risk of attacking blind.

Direct attacks

When your opponent controls no monsters at all, your monster can attack them directly. All of its ATK comes off their Life Points.

Common mistakes
  • Taking damage when your Defense Position monster is destroyed. You don't: only the monster goes.
  • Attacking with a monster in Defense Position. It has to be in Attack Position.
  • Switching position the turn a monster was summoned.

Recap

  • Upright is Attack Position (ATK counts), sideways is Defense Position (DEF counts).
  • Attack against attack: the weaker monster is destroyed and its owner takes the gap.
  • Attack against defense: no damage to the defender's owner, but a higher DEF hurts the attacker's owner.
  • No monsters means a direct attack for full ATK.
