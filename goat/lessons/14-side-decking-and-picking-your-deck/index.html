Side Decking and Picking Your Deck

Changing your deck between games, and choosing one to learn. 9 min · 5 flashcards

What the Side Deck is for

After the first game of a match you know what your opponent plays. Your 15 Side Deck cards let you swap in answers to it, one card in for one card out.

Four steps to a Side Deck

goatformat.com's side decking guide lays out a method in four steps:

  1. List what can come out. Every Main Deck card that could leave in games 2 and 3.
  2. List your hard matchups. Note how often you expect to face each one.
  3. List answers, then cut. Drop answers that clash with your own deck or don't do enough.
  4. Write a chart. For each matchup, what comes in and what goes out, and whether you are going first or second.

Watch out for clashes

A card that is great in one deck can hurt another. The guide's example is : strong against Burn, but in a Warrior deck full of Traps it would shut off your own cards.

Flexible decks like Goat Control can swap many cards. Decks with one narrow plan have fewer slots to change.

Another approach: change decks entirely

Some players side all 15 cards into a different deck for games 2 and 3, for example from Goat Control into Thunder Dragon Chaos. Your opponent sides for the deck they saw, so their answers miss. The risk, as the guide notes, is that you give up your answers to combo decks.

Picking your first deck

YGOPRODeck's introduction calls Goat Control the best deck and the one a new player should pick up, while warning that it takes a lot of skill to master. It also suggests keeping one-turn kill and alternate win decks for fun rather than as your main deck. Whatever you choose, play one deck many times. Knowing your own deck well matters more than having the best one.

Recap

  • Side cards swap in one for one between games.
  • Build it in four steps: what can come out, hard matchups, answers, a chart.
  • Watch for side cards that clash with your own deck.
  • Start with Goat Control, and stick with one deck while you learn.
