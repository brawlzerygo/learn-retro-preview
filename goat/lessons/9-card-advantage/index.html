Card Advantage

Trading one for one, finding free pluses, and reading your opponent. 10 min · 6 flashcards

Goat is a numbers game

Games in Goat are long. A single game can run close to 20 turns, and big swings are rare. So the question every turn is: after this play, will I have more cards than my opponent, or fewer? Count the cards in your hand plus the cards on your field. Whoever holds more good cards is usually the one who wins.

Think of it like this

Each card is a coin. A good trade swaps one of your coins for one of theirs. A great play gets you two of theirs for one of yours, or a coin for nothing.

One-for-one

Most plays are one-for-one: you spend a card to remove one of theirs. Sakuretsu Armor for an attacker, Mystical Space Typhoon for a Set card. Neither player gets ahead. Goat players trade like this for many turns and wait for a chance to get ahead.

Plus one

A plus one (+1) is any play that leaves you a card ahead. Some cards do it on their own:

CardDescription
One card in, two cards out.
One card, two of theirs discarded (if they hold two or more).
Destroys a Set card and stays on the field.
Trades itself away, then fetches a monster from your Deck.
Their monster becomes your monster.
Magician of FaithFlips and returns a Spell, so it is two cards from one.

According to YGOPRODeck's introduction, getting more than one card ahead in a single turn rarely happens in Goat, and it often decides the game when it does.

Reading your opponent

  • Their discards talk. If a Graceful Charity sends away a LIGHT and a DARK monster, a Chaos monster may be coming.
  • Set cards on a turn they could have attacked often mean a Trap that stops attacks.
  • Count what is left. Most staples are Limited. Once you have seen their Mirror Force, there isn't another.

Playing around Traps

Make a smaller play first to draw out a Trap, then make the big one. goatformat.com's article on against Snatch Steal makes the same point from the other side: you don't have to answer a card the moment it is played. Sometimes it is better to wait until your opponent has spent more on the play, and then break it.

Common mistakes
  • Chasing Life Points instead of cards. Damage matters at the end, cards matter all game.
  • Using a removal card on a small threat, then having nothing for the big one.
  • Dumping your hand onto the field into a possible Torrential Tribute or Mirror Force.

Recap

  • Count hand plus field to see who is ahead.
  • One-for-one trades keep things even. A plus one puts you ahead.
  • Pot of Greed, Delinquent Duo, Breaker, Sangan, Snatch Steal and Magician of Faith all gain cards.
  • Read discards and Set cards, and bait Traps before your big play.
